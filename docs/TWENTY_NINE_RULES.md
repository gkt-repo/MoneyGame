# MoneyGame 29 — single-deal-v1

This is a named MoneyGame house ruleset, not a universal version of 29. One deal of eight tricks settles the entry pool. There are no practice bots, marriage/pair adjustments, doubles, redoubles, or single-hand variants.

- Four players sit in seats 0–3. Seats 0 and 2 are partners; seats 1 and 3 are partners. The first dealer is seat 0. Turns proceed through increasing seat numbers, wrapping to 0.
- The deck has 32 unique cards. Each suit ranks J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7 from strongest to weakest. J scores 3, 9 scores 2, A and 10 score 1 each; other cards score 0. There are 28 card points and no last-trick bonus.
- Deal four cards per player before bidding. The player after the dealer bids first. Bids are whole numbers from 16 through 28 and must exceed the previous bid. Passing removes that player from the auction. When only one bidder remains, that player takes the highest contract. If everybody passes without a bid, rotate the dealer one seat and deal a fresh deck; no additional entry fee is charged.
- The contractor privately selects any of the four suits as trump. Deal the remaining four cards to each player. The contractor leads the first trick; subsequent tricks are led by the previous trick's winner.
- A player must follow the lead suit if able. Otherwise they may discard any card or, while trump is hidden, reveal trump. Only the current player who cannot follow suit may reveal; revealing while leading is not allowed. A player who reveals must play a trump if they hold one. On later turns, a player unable to follow suit may discard even if they hold trump.
- A card acts as trump only if played after trump has been revealed. Cards already played before revelation are not upgraded retroactively. Highest effective trump wins; if no effective trump was played, highest card of the lead suit wins.
- After eight tricks, the contractor's partnership wins if its collected points reach the contract. Otherwise the defending partnership wins.
- The server applies the configured platform commission to the entry pool, rounded to the nearest paise. The two winning partners split the remainder. If an odd paise remains, the winner in the lower numbered seat receives it. Both players receive their individual result and payout. Settlement is atomic and retryable; a settlement delay does not mean payment has completed.
- Each turn lasts 20 seconds. A disconnected player receives a single 30-second grace allowance per deal: at disconnect if it is their turn, or when their next turn begins. Reconnecting repeatedly cannot renew it. Timeout bidding passes; timeout trump selection picks the most frequent suit in the first hand (ties: Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades); timeout card play uses the first server-listed legal card in hand order. These reliability actions are not practice bots.
- If all four players remain disconnected for five minutes, the next timeout cancels the table and refunds every entry. An administrator can also cancel an active table for a full entry refund. A completed settlement cannot subsequently be cancelled.
- Accepted actions have a durable sequence. Stale requests are rejected; retries of the most recent identical action are acknowledged without applying it again. A restart restores the saved deal and deadline, or resumes pending settlement. An invalid legacy snapshot is cancelled and refunded rather than used to play an inconsistent deck.
